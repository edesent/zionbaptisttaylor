*A Devotional on Ephesians 1:15–23*

Paul prayed for a small church in a city full of powers. Ephesus had its goddess, its magicians, and its loyal subjects of Caesar, and the believers there were badly outnumbered.

He didn't ask God to clear those powers out of the city. He asked God to open the believers' eyes, "the eyes of your understanding being enlightened" (**Eph. 1:18**), so they could see the One seated above every one of them.

What did he want them to see? The Father raised Jesus from the dead and "seated Him at His right hand in the heavenly places, far above all principality and power and might and dominion, and every name that is named" (**Eph. 1:20–21**). The same power that emptied the tomb now works "toward us who believe" (**v. 19**).

Then Paul added the phrase that should send us home rejoicing. God "gave Him to be head over all things to the church" (**v. 22**). The King rules everything, and He rules it for His people.

Polycarp, the aged pastor of Smyrna, understood this around AD 155. When a Roman proconsul ordered him to curse Christ, he answered, "Fourscore and six years have I been His servant, and He hath done me no wrong. How then can I blaspheme my King who saved me?"

This week, name the power that looks largest in your life. Then pray Paul's prayer, lift your eyes, and remember where your King sits.
